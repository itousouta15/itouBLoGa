# Am I the Chief Organizer? I'm So Done

Because I did not properly consider the situation before starting the event, and because things kept going wrong along the way, I am now completely exhausted.

From the beginning, it was just a few people’s idea, but once we started preparing the summer training, I realized that the role of "chief organizer" is completely different from what I imagined.

I thought it would be enough to run the event well and get everyone together, but in reality, there are far more things to handle than I expected.

Venue, curriculum, budget, cooperation, promotion, team communication, schedule planning...
Many things do not produce results just because you try hard.

Sometimes I work very seriously and still get stuck;
sometimes I send messages and receive no reply for a long time;
sometimes I only want to do the event well, but I start to doubt whether I am fit to be the chief organizer.

I am not actually very good at management.
Many times, I do things while learning, or even force myself to keep going.

Sometimes after a meeting, I come home and my head is still thinking about the next step;
sometimes just seeing the to-do list makes me suddenly feel very tired.

I used to think that organizing an event was just "producing something," but after actually starting, I found that the hardest part was never the technology. It was communication and coordination between people, and all kinds of unpredictable emergencies.

Some things you think are arranged, suddenly change the next moment;
Some problems you think are solved, reappear after a few days.

And the scariest thing is,
many times as the chief organizer there is no real "clocking out."

Notifications keep popping up on my phone,
my mind keeps turning,
and even when I try to rest, I am still thinking about how to design the curriculum and other issues.

To be honest, I am now on the verge of collapse.

The first thing I do every day when I wake up is check messages,
my mind feels like it has never truly rested,
the to-do list never ends,
and problems keep coming one after another.

Sometimes I really wonder:

"Am I not fit to be the chief organizer?"
"Should I never have taken this on in the first place?"

I don't know what it will look like in the end.
I also don't know whether the final result will meet everyone's expectations.

As for how it will turn out...
I don't know either.

But there will probably be a next chapter to this article.
