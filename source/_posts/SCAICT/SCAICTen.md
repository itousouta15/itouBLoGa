A Brief Reflection on SCAICT Lately, from the President’s Perspective  
<!-- more -->

## About SCAICT

The Student Club's Association of Information in Central Taiwan (SCAICT) was first conceived on December 7, 2021, by Professor Hsin-Ming Cheng and Professor Chun-Ying Huang as part of the AIS3 new-style practical cybersecurity course. With support from You-Kang Chou of Wenhua High School and Cheng-Hsi Tsai of Taichung First Senior High School, it was officially founded in February 2022. Its goal is to integrate resources across central Taiwan and achieve sustainable continuity through information education and events.

## Ideals Are Great, but What About Reality?

As a “Student Club’s Association of Information” student clubs should be the foundation of our operations. But as the organization has aged, distortions emerged during handovers. The real needs of clubs were diluted across generations, eventually causing a disconnect between the organization and the grassroots. Activities became detached from demand, clubs shut down one by one, and the source of contributors dried up. No one wanted this to happen, but while we moved forward slowly, our core values quietly drifted off course. Our events could no longer reach the grassroots, and we even lost contact as information clubs in central Taiwan shrank.

In previous discussions with Fearnot, I never believed central Taiwan had no issue with unequal resource distribution. Resources here have always been in an awkward position. We don’t have the dense communities of the north, nor the strong cohesion of the south. Many clubs are struggling to survive. However, the problem is not only with the clubs themselves. There are also unaddressed internal challenges within SCAICT, and the most prominent one is the lack of a clear long-term organizational direction.

## What Should We Actually Be Doing?

SCAICT once thrived, and now it is going through decline. When the baton was passed to me, what I received was a sloppy to-do list and instructions to “carry on as usual.” I was told countless things we “had to do,” but no one ever told me *why*.

We arranged many short-term activities, but few people thought about the meaning and direction behind them. If we cannot answer “Why are we doing these things?”, then every effort risks becoming routine work without depth or lasting impact. Members’ sense of participation and responsibility gradually fades, and the next team is likely to just copy old patterns without understanding the organization’s true purpose. Short-term bustle hides long-term ambiguity, leaving the whole organization like a rudderless ship, busy but directionless.

After all this circling around, the core problem remains unsolved: what is the essence of our existence? If SCAICT exists only to run events, then where is our soul? Beyond maintaining the status quo, what real value can we create for student clubs in central Taiwan?

## Am I Really Doing a Good Job?

I’ve asked myself this question countless times over the five months since taking over SCAICT.

I have to admit: I haven’t done well in leadership. Whether it’s tedious paperwork, digital asset development, or overall community operations, I fell into a bad cycle: I kept taking everything onto myself.

I always felt I could do things faster alone, so I instinctively held onto every burden. But looking back, that approach was actually selfish. I kept myself so busy that I had no time to think about the bigger picture, while teammates who wanted to help were left waiting, watching their sense of involvement disappear bit by bit. Sometimes, when I looked at decisions full of holes, even I didn’t know what I was so busy with.

I’m still learning to let go of that obsession that “it has to be me.” I used to think carrying everything alone meant being responsible, but it doesn’t. I’m learning to trust the team, and to admit I cannot hold everything up by myself.

## Where Should We Go from Here?

**Is SCAICT doing okay?  
Honestly, not really.**

You could even say it has basically lost the meaning of its existence.

Sometimes I wonder whether the issue isn’t the external environment, but that we ourselves can no longer clearly explain why we should keep going.

SCAICT once had a reason to exist. That reason may never have been formally written down, but everyone knew what it was doing and who it was for. Looking back now, I find that even this has become blurred. Events are still happening, and people are still in the group chats, but that sense of necessity is no longer obvious.

So what’s next?

Honestly, I still don’t know the answer.

I only know that if we keep things exactly as they are and change nothing, it will keep fading away. No dramatic collapse, just fewer people mentioning it, less and less presence, until one day it disappears and no one is particularly surprised.

Perhaps what we truly need to face is not choosing a path, but honestly acknowledging our current state. Admitting that we are lost, admitting that we have no consensus, admitting that we also doubt ourselves.

But I still believe that as long as someone is willing to seriously think about this, it isn’t over.

Reform doesn’t have to be dramatic. It may simply mean answering a basic question again: do we still want it to exist? If the answer is yes, then the direction ahead should be decided by more people together, not carried by one person alone.

At least, as long as we can still discuss it, it means it’s not too late.

## Lastly, About Continuity

During this period, I began rethinking what “continuity” really means.

Continuity should not just be handing over folders and Google Drive permissions.  
It definitely should not be irresponsibly dumping a mess onto the next team.  
If the next people taking over still don’t know *why* they are doing this,  
then it means our generation has not completed its task.

Maybe true continuity is also passing down our confusion,  
so those who come later know this:  
we questioned and reflected, instead of moving forward blindly.

At least then, they can start from thinking, not from chaos.
