Because I did not anticipate the situation before organizing it,
and everything has gone wrong along the way,
I am now physically and mentally exhausted.

What started as just a few people's idea became a summer camp planning process, and only then did I realize that the role of “chief organizer” is completely different from what I had imagined.

I originally thought that as long as I could run the event well and bring everyone together, it would be enough. In reality, there are far more things to handle than I expected.

Venue, curriculum, budget, collaboration, promotion, team communication, schedule arrangement...
Many things do not necessarily have results just because you work hard.

Sometimes even though I have been pushing forward seriously, I still get stuck;
sometimes I send a message and receive no reply for a long time;
sometimes I just want to make the event good, but I start to doubt whether I am fit to be the chief organizer.

I am actually not very good at management.
Many times, I am only learning as I go, even forcing myself to keep going.

Sometimes after a meeting I come home and my mind is already thinking about the next step;
sometimes just seeing the to-do list suddenly makes me feel exhausted.

I used to think that organizing an event was just “producing something,”
but after actually starting, I discovered that the hardest part is never the technology; it is communication and coordination between people, and all kinds of unpredictable emergencies.

Some things you think are arranged suddenly change a second later;
some problems you thought were solved reappear after a few days.

And the scariest thing is,
being the chief organizer often means there is no real “off duty.”

My phone keeps buzzing with notifications,
my mind keeps racing,
and even when I rest, I still think about how to design the curriculum.

Honestly, I am already close to the edge of collapse.

The first thing I do every day when I wake up is check messages,
my mind feels like it never truly rested, my to-do list is never finished, and problems keep coming one after another.

Sometimes I really doubt:

"Am I not fit to be the chief organizer?"
"Should I never have accepted this in the first place?"

I do not know what I will end up becoming.
I also do not know whether the final result will meet everyone's expectations.

As for how it will end...
I do not know.

But this article will probably have a next chapter.
